---
title: Better Manage Your Playlists in Apple Music
date: 2026-07-26
---

In Apple Music (AM for short), there are three main ways to organize your music: **Library**, **Favorite Songs**, and **Playlists**.

Your Library is your personal music collection—adding a song to your Library makes it easy to find later. Favorite Songs and Playlists are both built from your Library. If you really like a particular song, you can mark it as a favorite. You can also create playlists based on your own preferences to categorize the songs in your Library.

One thing to note: when you add a song or album to a playlist, it will also be added to your Library—unless you turn off this sync option in Settings.

![image.png](https://blog-images.luckyabner.top/2026/07/bfafbf281e998dc2a6ce6e402a264dae.png)

I turned off playlist syncing to avoid accidentally adding songs I don't like to my Library when saving other people's playlists.

My current workflow is simple: when I hear a song I like, I add it to my Library. If I really love it, I mark it as a favorite.

On desktop, AM also offers a very handy feature called **Smart Playlists**. You can define your own rules, and the playlist will automatically filter songs from your Library that match those rules. It's incredibly convenient.

![image.png](https://blog-images.luckyabner.top/2026/07/c244a96f6db1dc977538e5adbd59530f.png)

Here's an example: back when I used NetEase Cloud Music, I simply split my playlists into "Chinese Songs" and "Other Languages." Whenever I heard a great Chinese song, I had to both mark it as a favorite and add it to the Chinese playlist manually. But with AM, I just add it to my Library, create a Smart Playlist, and it automatically filters out all Chinese songs and syncs in real time. Much less hassle.
